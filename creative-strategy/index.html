Study guide p. 5
The shift to short-form video
  • Video engages more, especially vertical 9:16 video with sound, for brand and direct response alike.
  • The first 2 to 3 seconds decide whether people keep watching. Establish the brand and message early.
  • Sound on and sound off coexist, so tell the story visually too: text overlays and captions.
  • Vertical, full-screen formats can beat landscape or square, especially in Reels and Stories.

One language for every team

Step 2 of 6

Campaigns and delivery

Creative performance depends on how campaigns are built and how the delivery system learns. Structure for flexibility, understand what creative changes in the auction, and give the system good signals.

Consolidate, don’t fragment

Every split into separate ad sets and budgets stops the system from finding the cheapest results across all audiences and creative. A consolidated structure lets it:

  • Move budget to the creative with the lowest-cost results.
  • Test more variations on more people, so signals arrive faster.
More detail
  • Avoid paying for results in one ad set when cheaper ones exist in another.

Consolidated campaigns need more creative variations, not fewer, so media and creative teams must plan together.

Study guide p. 16
Total value wins
Total value=bid×estimated action rate+ad quality

The ad with the highest total value wins the auction. Higher total value makes ads more competitive, which can lower costs.

ComponentCreative’s influence
Estimated action ratesStrong: clear value, relevant imagery and a strong call to action raise them

Study guide p. 18
What lowers ad quality
  • Misleading experiences from ad to landing page
  • Too many ads relative to content
  • Sexually suggestive or shocking content
  • Malicious or deceptive ads; pop-up interstitials
Study guide p. 18
What signals are

Data points the system uses to pick which creative to show each person, in which format and placement:

  • Audience characteristics: age, gender, language, past engagement.
  • Location: geography, local relevance, nearby stores.
More detail
  • Interests: topics, Pages, creators, content categories.
  • Behavioral context: time of day, device, connection speed, recent activity.

Step 3 of 6

Audiences and placements

Audience size decides how much work the creative must do. Pair broad audiences with diverse creative, then fit assets to every placement.

Two approaches
Narrow audienceBroad audience
HowYou set demographics, interests, behaviorsMinimal settings; the system finds responders
AdvantageHighly tailored creativeDiverse creative lets the system match each person
Trade-offFewer people see the messageEach asset feels less tailored (Advantage+ creative helps)
BriefOne focused messageMany messages, styles and value propositions
Study guide p. 27
Reach versus relevance

Narrowing the audience raises relevance but limits reach. Broadening it shifts the job of relevance from targeting to creative. Broad audiences with diverse, high-quality assets get both.

Study guide p. 31
Advantage+ and lookalikes
  • Advantage+ audience: your suggestions plus AI; works for almost all campaigns.
  • Lookalike audiences: a source of at least 100 people from one country, ideally 1,000+; size 1% to 10%, with 1% the closest match.
  • Custom audiences: people who already know the business; you see only the approximate size.
  • Advantage+ catalog ads: for broad audiences or for retargeting.
Study guide pp. 28–29
Best practices
  • Avoid overlap: fewer ad sets reaching the same people; use exclusions.
  • Combine audiences into fewer ad sets unless you need different cost controls.
  • Exclude existing and loyal customers when growing the customer base.
  • Avoid very narrow audiences: aim for at least 50 results per ad set per week.
Study guide pp. 30–31
One creative, every placement
Study guide pp. 33–35
Advantage+ or manual

Advantage+ placements (formerly automatic) show ads across Facebook, Messenger, Instagram and Audience Network: better results, saved time, extended reach. Manual placements give control, but every placement you opt out of raises costs.

Study guide p. 33
Three Reels ad formats
Study guide p. 36
Asset customization

Tailor assets per placement so ads run in as many places as possible:

  1. A product image on Facebook and a lifestyle image on Instagram.
  2. Sound-on video for in-stream, captioned sound-off video elsewhere.
  3. Creative matched to audience demographics.

Step 4 of 6

Creative diversification

More creative volume gives the delivery system more options; more options mean better matching, lower costs and better results. Vary formats, messages and styles, and keep assets fresh.

The principle

Different people respond to different formats, messages and styles. A diverse set lets the system:

  • Match format to preference: video, images or carousels.
  • Match message to motivation: problem-aware early, product-specific close to purchase.
  • Reduce fatigue by rotating a larger set.
  • Generate more signals, one stream per variation.
Study guide p. 39
Two dimensions
  1. Creative assets: high-quality video, static images, carousels and more, each telling the value proposition from a different angle.
  2. Placements: Advantage+ placements or 6 or more manual placements. Campaigns on 6+ placements are significantly more cost-efficient than those on fewer than 4.

Step 5 of 6

Mobile-first creative

On a phone the opening matters more than the ending. Front-load the brand and message, design for sound off and safe zones, and evaluate every asset against the checklist.

The opening is everything

If creative doesn’t grab attention in the first 2 to 3 seconds, it is less likely to be seen at all. Unlike TV storytelling, mobile creative front-loads the most important information: the first frame is the whole opportunity.

Study guide p. 47
Five first-frame strategies
Study guide p. 47
Video and branding
  • Mix images and video; even simple animation works.
  • Keep video under 15 seconds when possible; use in-stream with sound for longer, complex messages.
  • Show the brand, logo or product in the first 3 seconds and again at the end; use brand colors throughout.
Study guide p. 48
Sound on and off

Many Feed and Stories videos play muted; many reels play with sound. Make both work: captions, text overlays and graphics, branded product shots, and visuals that make sense without sound, plus music or effects for sound-on viewers.

Study guide p. 49
Text lengths and sizes
Study guide pp. 50, 52
Keep key elements in the safe zone
Study guide p. 51
Images, ratio and CTA
  • High-resolution, not over-edited; show the brand and the product in use; crop to the focus.
  • Fill as much of the vertical screen as possible; edit existing footage into vertical if needed.
  • Try different call-to-action buttons to see which work.
Study guide pp. 50–53
Templates and flexible format
  • Video templates turn images into mobile-first video; story templates make images vertical and add movement.
  • Flexible ad format: up to 10 images and videos; the system picks single image, video or carousel per person. Sales and app promotion objectives only.

Step 6 of 6

Evaluation and measurement

Measure outcomes that matter, test creative before, during and after campaigns, and turn every result into the next hypothesis.

What a learning agenda holds

A structured plan of the questions to answer, how to answer them and how to apply the answers:

  • Hypotheses about creative, audiences or effectiveness.
  • Measurement methods: tools, tests and metrics.
  • Timeline for data collection and analysis.
  • Action plan for applying learnings.
Study guide p. 60
Questions to ask
  1. Do my metrics measure business outcomes, or social metrics?
  2. If they are proxies, do they correlate with outcomes?
  3. Am I optimizing toward the outcome and objective?
  4. Which data sources feed my outcome metrics?
  5. Is the campaign set up for testing?
Study guide p. 61
Social, brand or business?

Select a metric, then its type.

Study guide p. 61
Why the difference matters

Highly engaging creative isn’t necessarily effective creative. Judge awareness creative by ad recall and awareness lift, consideration by link clicks and landing page views, and conversion creative by purchases, revenue and ROAS. Social metrics are early signals, not proof of impact.

Study guide p. 62
Before, during and after
Study guide pp. 63–67
Set up A/B tests

Random, non-overlapping groups; identical ads except one variable; the variation with the better cost per result wins. Create tests from the reporting table, in Experiments, or while creating a campaign. Test format, messaging, audiences or placements.

Study guide pp. 63–64
Data sources for real outcomes

Set up the Conversions API, Pixel and SDK to measure purchases, sign-ups, leads, offline conversions and app events, rather than proxies.

Brief in five
  1. Business goal (SMART, with a purpose statement)
  2. Campaign objective (1 of 6)
  3. Audience selection (broad, with varied creative)
  4. Measurement strategy (business outcomes, not proxies)
  5. Creative concept (works across formats and media)
Study guide p. 10
Common traps
  • Consolidated campaigns need more creative, not less.
  • Creative affects estimated action rates and ad quality, never the bid.
  • Engagement bait creates low-quality signals.
  • Social metrics are not proof of business impact.
  • Flexible format works only with sales and app promotion objectives.
  • To test a full-length versus edited video, use an A/B test, not week-on-week comparisons.
